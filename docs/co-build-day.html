Run a co-build day.

One day, one team, one use case. Teams agree on what to build in the morning and deliver it in the afternoon.

Application types Worked example Co-build day Participant requirements Discovery session

A co-build day takes a team from an idea to a reviewed application in its own Azure sandbox. The morning is spent agreeing on what to build; the afternoon is spent delegating the build and checking the result. Each team works on one use case in its own repository, following the path.

Before the day

Ask each team for its own use case at least a week ahead. A team's own problem keeps people engaged, and what they build is still useful after the day. Use the worked example only for a team that has no use case, can't use its data yet, or wants to learn the flow first.

For each team, agree:

  • The use case. One user, one journey, one decision or task the app helps with. Small enough to build in an afternoon.
  • The people. Someone who knows the business process and can answer questions, someone who will use the app, and an owner who can approve the architecture.
  • The data. Synthetic data, or real data with written approval from its owner. Default to synthetic.
  • The sandbox. One Azure resource group per team, with permission to create Azure Container Apps and Microsoft Foundry resources, and a named person who approves what gets deployed.
  • The tools. GitHub Copilot with the model and plugin or skills listed in Start. See participant requirements.

If the business context is spread across several people, run a discovery session before the day.

The morning matters most. If time runs short, cut design or deployment, not discovery or architecture.

Rules for the room

  • Synthetic data unless the data owner approved real data in writing.
  • The AI suggests; a named person decides. If the app uses AI, its output is a proposal that a person accepts or rejects.
  • Agree before you build. No implementation before the architecture is approved and the five questions are all yes.
  • Deploy only to the team's sandbox, after its owner approves the resource list.

Facilitating

Open with the talk. Present it from the Why page, or download the PDF there.

During the morning, watch for teams that skim the creator's questions to get to code. Ask them to show you the swimlane and explain who does each step. If they can't, they aren't ready to confirm discovery.

At each gate, ask who approved it. The answer should be a person in the room, not the agent.

In the afternoon, the common failure is the agent making a decision the package didn't. When a team says "it decided to…", send them back to agree it and update the specification.

At show and tell, ask each team what the diagrams or the prototype caught before the build. That's the evidence that the practice worked.
